About

We saw the leak before we built the tool.

ELFALIGNED takes the repetitive work off your desk — the tasks nobody wants to do — and governs the AI so it stays safe. Two decades running a small business taught us what's actually worth automating.

Who's behind it

Twenty-two years in a family tractor dealership, before any of this.

I spent 22 years in a family tractor dealership in northern Michigan. I've sat in your chair — watched the ad budget leak, learned what actually worked in a town this size. Nobody from a marketing agency can say that.

A dealership teaches you where the hours really go: the quote that took two days to assemble, the inventory that never quite matched, the follow-up that lived in one person's memory, the same question answered for the hundredth time. Those are exactly the tasks we automate now.

The approach here is deliberately narrow and supervised. Automation runs inside a structured workflow with a human checking the output — and it's priced before it's built, with a plan you can say no to.

The work is remote and documented in writing, because that's how it has to be and because it's better anyway. You get documents you can check, forward, and hold me to.

Our mission

ELFALIGNED automates the mundane — the repetitive tasks nobody wants to do — and governs the AI so it stays safe. Not new operating systems: the copy-paste workflows, the manual bridges, the approvals that happen over text.

We start by looking at how your operation actually runs — where the hours go, what gets re-typed, what process lives in one person's head. Then we automate the parts that genuinely pay, keep your existing software in place, and wrap it in oversight: who approved it, where the data goes, what it costs.

Local AI is available where cloud dependence would be a liability rather than a feature. The data you send us stays on our systems.

Every engagement ends the same way: a detailed plan and a quote to implement, priced before anything is built, so you know exactly what you are buying and what it costs before a dollar moves.
