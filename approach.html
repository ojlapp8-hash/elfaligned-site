Approach

Data in, written plan out. And it gets checked.

Most consulting fails before a recommendation is written — because nobody measured the real numbers, and nobody checked the result. Our process fixes both, and it never requires a site visit.

The four steps, in detail

Same sequence every time. Whether it's a governance audit, an automation, or a marketing study, this is how we get there.

  1. 1 · Study how your operation actually runs

    No call needed to start. You send what describes the work: the spreadsheets, the exports, the process notes, the logs of who does what by hand. The better the picture we have, the more we can solve — and the less guesswork ends up in the quote.

    We read it for the pattern where repetitive work hides: which step gets re-typed, which process changes hands twice, which list exists because the real one is unusable, and which question comes through for the hundredth time.

    You get: a written read on where the repetitive work actually is, ranked by what it costs you, usually within two business days. If what you sent isn't enough to work from, we'll name the two or three specific things that would make it enough.

  2. 2 · Pick what's worth automating

    Not everything should be automated, and a one-off task never should. We recommend the specific repetitive tasks worth handing to a supervised agent — typically in a defined lane like booking, lead follow-up, CRM updates, document processing, or answering repeat questions.

    You get: a shortlist of what to automate and what to leave alone — each one sized in hours saved or cost avoided. This step is often the most valuable, because it stops you spending money automating things that aren't the real drag.

  3. 3 · Deploy a supervised agent — or buy off the shelf

    Sometimes the answer is software that already exists, configured properly. Sometimes nothing fits and we build an agent for it. Either way the plan comes to you in writing, with the cost, before anything is approved. Nobody quietly decides to build something else.

    You get: a defined automation that a human checks. We build the agent around your existing software — it stays, we add the layer. If the honest answer is "buy this off-the-shelf product instead," we write that down and tell you which one.

  4. 4 · Deliver the plan and quote, then governance

    Every engagement ends the same way: a detailed plan and a quote to implement, so you know exactly what you're buying and what it costs before a dollar moves. The automation is delivered through supervised agentic bots configured from your own data.

    The person who built it doesn't get to decide it works. A separate check compares the finished work against what we agreed in step three. If it doesn't pass, it goes back, and you hear about it from us rather than discovering it later.

    You get: the automation, the oversight, and everything documented — so it runs with a human checking the output, or it runs without us, for as long as you want.

Principles

Five rules we don't break.

Data-driven and evidence-based

Recommendations come from your numbers, not from a deck full of buzzwords. If we can't show the evidence, we don't make the claim.

Honest about existing solutions

When a good tool already exists, we use it. We're not quick to reinvent what already works just because building is what we do.

You own the output

The plan, the tools, the documentation — all transfer to you. Nothing reverts, nothing has to be re-rented.

Plain language, in writing

If we can't explain what we're recommending in words a shop owner uses, we don't understand it well enough to recommend it.

Honest "no"

If technology is the wrong answer, we say so on day one and charge you for the analysis only.
