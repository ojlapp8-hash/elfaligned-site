How we benchmark

Knowing you're behind is useless without knowing behind what.

A number on its own tells you nothing. A 3% close rate could be excellent or terrible depending on what's normal for your sector, in your region, at your size.

So we research successful businesses in similar sectors and regions, find out what good actually looks like where you operate, and put your numbers next to it. That's how you find out whether a gap is a real problem or a strength you didn't know you had.

Sector comparison

How businesses like yours perform on the measures that matter — margins, turnaround, customer retention, cost per job.

Regional comparison

What's achievable in your market specifically. A rural shop and a metro one don't compete on the same numbers.

Gap, sized and ranked

Every gap comes with what it costs you now and what closing it is worth — not a list of vague improvements.

How solutions are delivered

Specialized agentic bots, built from your data — not a template.

Off-the-shelf software makes your business fit the product. We build the other way around: specialized agents configured from your own records, your processes, and your numbers, so the solution fits how you actually operate.

Built from your records

Each agent is configured against your data — your pricing history, your customer patterns, your process steps. Nothing is inferred from a generic profile.

Your data stays on our systems

You send us your records and they stay where we work on them — they don't get forwarded or stored anywhere else. Local AI is available where cloud dependence would be a liability rather than a feature.

Existing tool or custom build

When a good tool already exists, we use it. When nothing fits, we build it. We're not in the business of reinventing what already works.

Local AI, where it matters

Cloud dependence is sometimes a liability, not a feature.

For plenty of businesses, a cloud service is the right call and we'll say so. For others — regulated work, sensitive customer data, rural connections, operations that can't afford an outage — running AI locally is the difference between a solution and a new problem.

Runs where the data lives

Processing happens on the machine or server that holds the records, rather than getting sent to a third-party cloud to be processed and stored.

Works when the connection doesn't

No outage window. A rural shop with flaky broadband gets the same reliability as a downtown office with fiber.

An option, not a doctrine

Local AI where security and uptime matter; cloud where it genuinely fits better. We recommend the right one and explain why.

How we work

Four steps. Same sequence every time.

Whether it's a governance audit, an automation, or a marketing study, the process doesn't change. Nothing depends on a meeting.

  1. Study how your operation actually runs

    You send what describes the work — the spreadsheet, the export, the process notes, the logs of who does what by hand. The better the picture we have, the more we can solve, and the less guesswork goes into the quote.

    We read it for the pattern where repetitive work hides: which step gets re-typed, which process changes hands twice, which question comes through for the hundredth time.

  2. Pick what's worth automating

    Not everything should be automated, and a one-off task never should. We put your numbers next to what good looks like and recommend the specific repetitive tasks worth handing to a supervised agent — each one sized in hours saved or cost avoided.

  3. Deploy a supervised agent — or buy off the shelf

    Sometimes the answer is software that already exists, configured properly. Sometimes nothing fits and we build an agent for it. Either way you get it in writing, with the cost, before anything is approved.

  4. Deliver a plan and a quote, then governance

    Every engagement ends the same way: a detailed plan and a quote to implement, so you know exactly what you're buying and what it costs before a dollar moves. The automation is delivered through supervised agentic bots tailored to your data — and checked by someone who didn't build it.
